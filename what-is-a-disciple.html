FOUNDATIONAL STUDY 01

What Does It Really Mean to Be a Disciple of Jesus?

It is possible to participate in Christianity without ever seriously asking what Jesus expected of the people who followed Him. Church attendance, baptism, stopping cursing, listening to different music, and other changed habits can all have a place in a life of faith. But none of them, by themselves, answer the question Jesus puts before us.

Am I actually following Jesus?

FOLLOW

Jesus Called People to Follow Him.

Today the word Christian can describe many different things. In the Gospels, Jesus repeatedly used a more demanding picture. He called people to come after Him, learn from Him, remain with Him, obey Him, and become His disciples.

The New Testament certainly uses the word Christian. But the word disciple forces us to ask what belonging to Christ actually looks like.

“If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me.”Matthew 16:24

Jesus did not call people merely to identify with Him. He called them to follow Him.

A disciple begins by listening, learning, and staying near the Teacher.

COST

Jesus Did Not Hide the Cost.

Luke 14 records large crowds traveling with Jesus. Rather than making His invitation easier, Jesus turned and told them plainly what following Him meant. He spoke about competing loyalties, carrying the cross, and the complete surrender that comes with becoming His disciple.

“So therefore, any one of you who does not renounce all that he has cannot be my disciple.”Luke 14:33

Jesus' first disciples were given a mission that was uniquely theirs. They had walked with Him, heard His teaching, witnessed His miracles, and would become witnesses of His death and resurrection. Jesus prepared them to testify to what they had seen and heard, and promised that the Holy Spirit would bring His teaching to their remembrance and empower their witness after He departed. Their mission would carry the Gospel first among the Jews and then outward to the nations.

We do not share their unique place as eyewitnesses or apostles, but we do share the call to be disciples. Their testimony has been handed down to us, and the mission continues: make disciples of all nations, baptizing them and teaching them to obey everything Jesus commanded. We follow the same Lord, learn His ways, bear witness to the Gospel they proclaimed, and help others become His disciples.

The deeper claim therefore reaches every disciple: Jesus has become Lord. Nothing else can hold a higher claim on us than He does.

My life is no longer my own.

CROSS

They Knew What a Cross Meant.

For modern Christians, the cross is familiar. We see it on churches, jewelry, artwork, and Bibles. Jesus' first hearers knew it as an instrument of Roman execution. When He told people to take up their cross, the image carried the unmistakable language of death and surrender.

This does not mean a disciple must seek misery. It means following Jesus reaches deeper than changing a few outward habits. The old claim that I belong to myself is surrendered.

“We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death... we too might walk in newness of life.”Romans 6:4

Discipleship is not merely giving things up. It is receiving a new life and learning how to live it.

SURRENDER

Church Attendance Is Not the Same as Discipleship.

A person can attend church regularly, be baptized, stop swearing, change what they listen to, and adopt religious habits. Those things may accompany genuine transformation. But outward participation can also become a substitute for the much harder question of whether we are actually learning to obey and become like Christ.

Religious identity asks

Do I belong to the Christian group? Do I attend? Have I completed the expected religious steps?

A disciple asks

Am I following Jesus? Is He changing what I love, how I treat people, what I obey, and who I am becoming?

The goal is not to look more religious. The goal is to become like Christ.

SURRENDER

A Disciple Is Not a Second Kind of Christian.

It is easy to imagine two callings: ordinary Christians who believe in Jesus, and disciples who choose a more serious life of obedience. But the New Testament does not give us a comfortable category for belonging to Christ while continuing to live for our own ambitions, attitudes, priorities, and desires. To follow Jesus is to surrender our claim to them. We are learning to take up His ambitions, His attitudes, His priorities, and to hold dear what He holds dear. His ways become our ways because we now belong to Him.

This is what it means to live in His name. We are no longer building a life around ourselves and asking Jesus to bless it. We are learning to build our lives around Him.

“And he died for all, that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for him who for their sake died and was raised.”2 Corinthians 5:15

This matters because it is possible to place our confidence in the fact that we once prayed, were baptized, joined a church, or sincerely professed faith in Christ, while continuing to live largely for ourselves. Christ's sacrifice was never presented as a covering that allows us to remain willingly under the rule of sin while assuring ourselves that our relationship with God is secure. We are not saved because we have made ourselves righteous, and obedience does not purchase God's forgiveness. But when Christ becomes our Lord, we have changed masters. Sin may still tempt us, and we may still stumble, but we are no longer called to obey it, defend it, or arrange our lives around it. We belong to Christ, and our lives are now being brought under His rule.

“Little children, let no one deceive you. Whoever practices righteousness is righteous, as he is righteous.”1 John 3:7

John begins with a warning: “let no one deceive you.” A Christian can still sin, and John has already made that clear earlier in the letter. The difference is not that a disciple never falls. The difference is who now has the rightful claim on his life. Sin is no longer something to protect, excuse, or deliberately continue in as though Christ's sacrifice makes obedience unnecessary. When a disciple sins, he confesses it, turns from it, and returns to the One to whom he now belongs.

The question is not whether I once called myself a Christian. The question is whether I belong to Christ and am following Him.

ALLEGIANCE

Whose Mark Does Your Life Bear?

Scripture presents humanity as belonging to a master. We can be slaves to sin, or through Christ become servants of righteousness and servants of God. The question is not whether something shapes us. The question is who does.

“You who were once slaves of sin have become obedient from the heart...”Romans 6:17

Revelation uses striking imagery of allegiance. God's servants are sealed on their foreheads, while the beast's mark is placed on the forehead or right hand. Deuteronomy also uses forehead and hand imagery for God's commands shaping His people. The point is larger than a visible mark: whom do we belong to, and whose ways are shaping what we think and what we do?

Forehead · The Mind
What is shaping the way I think?

What do I believe? What occupies my thoughts? Whose values are becoming mine? A disciple learns to see the world increasingly through the teaching and character of Christ.

Hand · The Life
What is shaping what I do?

Whom do I obey? What do my choices reveal? Faith eventually reaches our hands, our speech, our relationships, our money, our mercy, our honesty, and the way we treat people.

A disciple should increasingly bear the family resemblance of Christ.

Remain

“If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples.” John 8:31

Love

“By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another.” John 13:35

Bear Fruit

“By this my Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit and so prove to be my disciples.” John 15:8

None of this means a disciple never stumbles. The evidence is not perfection. When sin appears, the disciple does not simply make peace with it as master. We repent, return to Christ, and keep following. Over time, the direction of the life begins to reveal whose we are.

You should not need a label for your life to eventually give away whom you follow.

GROWTH

New Life Begins Before Maturity Arrives.

A new Christian does not immediately become like Christ, even though that is the goal. God has begun His work in us, but disciples need time to learn Scripture, recognize sin, practice obedience, receive correction, develop wisdom, and grow in love.

New believers were never meant simply to be baptized and left to figure everything out alone. Mature Christians should walk beside them, teach them, model faithfulness, answer questions, and help them learn the way of Jesus.

“Go therefore and make disciples of all nations... teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you.”Matthew 28:19–20

Jesus made disciples, then commissioned disciples to make disciples.

At some point, what we have learned has to become the road we actually walk.

WITNESS

New Faith Should Burn Brightly.

A new believer does not need years of study before telling someone about Christ. After the woman at the well encountered Jesus, she immediately returned to her town and invited people to come see Him. Her understanding was still developing, but she could tell people about the One she had encountered.

“Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did. Can this be the Christ?”John 4:29

That excitement should be encouraged, not suppressed. A new believer can tell family, friends, and coworkers what Christ has done and invite them to discover Him too.

But sharing your testimony is different from being sent to teach, lead, or officially represent the church. New faith can also be fragile. A young disciple needs strengthening and protection while roots grow deep.

New believers need room to burn brightly without being asked to carry weight they are not ready to carry.

MATURITY

Every Disciple Is Called to Follow. Not Every Disciple Is Ready to Lead.

The New Testament places real weight on proven character, faithfulness, sound teaching, and maturity when responsibility for others is involved. Enthusiasm is valuable, but enthusiasm and maturity are not the same thing.

The first disciples themselves stumbled, misunderstood Jesus, and needed correction. But Jesus discipled them. He formed them before entrusting them with greater responsibility.

Churches should therefore be discerning about whom they send to teach or represent Christ in mission. A mission trip is not merely an experience for the person going. When someone is sent in the name of a church to proclaim Christ, other people may form their understanding of the Gospel through that person's words and life.

“He must not be a recent convert, or he may become puffed up with conceit...”1 Timothy 3:6

The immediate context concerns an overseer, but the principle is worth hearing: spiritual responsibility should not be confused with spiritual newness.

Maturity begins to bear fruit when a disciple can help another person walk the road.

THE PATH

Come. Follow. Grow. Help Others Follow.

There is no mechanical timetable for maturity. People grow at different rates, and every disciple remains dependent on God's grace. But the New Testament gives us a recognizable direction.

Come to Christ→Learn to Follow Christ→Grow in Christ→Help Others Follow Christ

This is not a ladder by which we earn heaven. It is the life of a disciple: receiving Christ, walking with Him, being changed by Him, and eventually becoming someone who can faithfully help another person walk the same road.

WEDDING

The Road Leads to the Marriage Supper of the Lamb.

Jesus often spoke of God's kingdom with the picture of a banquet or wedding feast. In His parables, people who were invited found reasons not to come. Some were too occupied with possessions, work, or ordinary life to recognize the importance of the invitation. The warning is sobering because an invitation can be heard and still refused.

Revelation brings that wedding picture to its great conclusion. The Bride has made herself ready, and the saints are pictured in fine linen. The final picture is not of people who merely adopted a Christian name. It is a people who belong to the Lamb.

“Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready. And to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints.”Revelation 19:7–8, KJV

The fine linen does not mean the saints purchased their place at the table by righteous works. The invitation, the Lamb, forgiveness, new life, and even the Bride's preparation all begin with God's grace. But grace does not make the life that follows meaningless. Revelation does not picture a Bride indifferent to the Bridegroom. She has made herself ready.

That is why counting the cost is not a gloomy addition to the Gospel. Jesus is worth the cost. He is not merely calling us away from an old life. He is calling us to Himself.

Do not make excuses when the invitation comes. Follow the One who invited you.

THE QUESTION TO TAKE WITH YOU

Am I Actually Following Jesus?

Not simply: Do I call myself a Christian? Do I attend church? Have I completed the expected religious steps? The deeper question is whether Jesus is truly my Lord and Teacher, and whether His way is increasingly becoming visible in the way I think, love, speak, choose, and live.
